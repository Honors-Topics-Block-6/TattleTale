# Minigames

## Email sorting

The player sees a small inbox with a few short, slightly chaotic messages and 3–5 folders like **Important**, **Events**, **Updates**, **Junk**, and **Later**. They open or preview each email and drag it into the best category. The tone should feel more like a student org, indie team, or messy online group than a corporation, so the messages are things like reminders, shared links, announcements, duplicate threads, and mildly useless updates.

## Calendar organization

The player sees a day or week view with a few pre-placed blocks and a handful of loose event cards on the side. They drag the cards into the correct open time slots, with very light constraints like “afternoon only” or “before study session.” The appeal is mostly visual and tactile: making a messy schedule neat.

## Vibecoding

The player is given a simple request for an AI builder, like making a profile page or cleaning up a homepage. The AI generates a rough version that gets some things wrong, and the player chooses from a few instruction options to tell it what to fix. It updates, still misses something, and after 2–3 rounds of correction the result becomes good enough to submit.

## Doomscrolling

The player sees a constantly scrolling social feed filled mostly with distracting junk. They can tap quick reactions on posts as they pass, but they must also save the few posts that are genuinely useful, informative, or relevant to the prompt. The challenge is keeping up with the feed without wasting attention on slop or missing the good posts.
